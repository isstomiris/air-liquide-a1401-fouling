Fans are not fins

Fans are the three big propellers at the bottom (2.4 m wide). They push outside air up.

Fins are thousands of thin aluminium rings wrapped around every tube, about 423 per metre of tube. They add surface so the air can take more heat from the gas. Each tube has 23 times more fin area than bare tube area.

The fin gap is the 2 mm space between two neighbouring rings on the same tube. Air has to squeeze through these gaps, and that is where fluff, pollen and insects get stuck.

What each part does

Fans + motors (A, B, C): push 342,000 m³/h of air up when clean; speed set by the outlet temperature controller.

Plenum: the empty box between fans and tubes. It spreads the air evenly. It is not the fin gap.

Tube bundle: 276 stainless tubes in 6 rows. Hot wet syngas flows inside, 2 passes (top rows first, then back through the bottom rows).

Headers: boxes at each end that turn the gas round and collect it.

Why debris hurts so much

The fans blow from below, so debris lands on the underside of the bottom row and wedges into the fin gaps.

Blocked gaps mean (1) more resistance, so the fans move less air, and (2) fewer fins doing any work. Both cut the heat the air can carry away.

Up to about 40% blockage the cooler copes. Past that the gas outlet temperature climbs fast, which is the "cliff" you can see with the slider.
