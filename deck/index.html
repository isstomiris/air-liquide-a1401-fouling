Air Liquide · Atyrau hydrogen plant · Case A-1401

The cooler that chokes every summer

Poplar fluff, insects and dust fill the 2 mm gaps between the fins of air cooler A-1401. Less air gets through, the gas leaves hotter, and the plant runs less gas on hot days.

01 What the cooler does
02 / 07

Hot, wet gas in. Fans push air up through the fins. Water condenses.

Most of the work is condensing water

About half to two-thirds of the heat removed is water vapour turning to liquid, not just gas cooling down. The separator D-1403 drains that water.

Air comes from below

Fluff and insects are carried up and stick on the underside of the bottom tube rows. Any screen or cleaner has to reach there.

Hot day = smaller margin

Warm air has less "temperature difference" to take heat. With clogged fins, even 100 % fans cannot keep up.

Sources: datasheet, PFD, diagnosis Part 1–2 (A-1401_PFD_and_thermal_diagnosis.md)

Real fouling moves slowly, about 2 points a month. So the forecast is no better than "no change", and 20–50 % better than a straight line. The real value is the health indicator and the alarm, which catch the summer rise as it happens.

05 Our two options
06 / 07

Same monitor, two ways to reopen the gaps

A

Monitoring + ultrasonic cleaning

SONICATION · EARLY AND OFTEN
  • Sound waves shake loose, fresh fluff off the fins
  • Runs at a low trigger, before deposits pack down
  • No water, no chemicals on hot 304L tubes
Trigger 30 %
model scale
B

Monitoring + dry projection

DECAMEX-LIKE · LESS OFTEN, DEEPER
  • Dry media blown through the fins removes compacted debris
  • Can wait longer, up to just before the "cliff"
  • No liquid left on the bundle
Trigger 40 %
model scale
Rejected

Water washing

Chlorides can crack 304L tubes (stress-corrosion), thermal shock on hot rows, freezing in winter, wet pollen turns to mud.

Chemical or foam cleaning

Liquid on hot tubes again, plus residues on the fins and waste to handle.

On the real-data scale (constant 50–60 % floor) the triggers become 63 % (A) and 66 % (B). Both are engineering assumptions the team can tune.
06 Open questions

Three things that would make the numbers sharper

?1

Inspect a freshly cleaned bundle

Explains the 50–60 % that never goes away: our model, louvres, fan pitch, or bent and corroded fins.

?2

Condensate flow from D-1403

The most direct way to measure how much heat the cooler removes. Today our duty estimate is 3.5–5.3 MW.

?3

Summer demand and fan current

Separates "the cooler limited us" from "customers needed less" in the H₂ estimate, and tests fan current as an extra signal.

In one line: the cooler is a real, recurring summer bottleneck; debris adds 10–15 points each season and cleaning removes it. A shared monitor decides when, and A or B does the cleaning.
